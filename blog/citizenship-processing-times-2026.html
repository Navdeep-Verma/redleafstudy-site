Citizenship Processing Times in 2026: Grant vs. Proof of Citizenship

Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

If you've seen headlines this year about a citizenship backlog stretching past a year, it's worth pausing before assuming it applies to you — IRCC actually runs two separate citizenship processes with two very different queues, and 2026's news has mostly been about the one that has nothing to do with the test most people preparing on this site are studying for.

Quick answer: If you're studying for the citizenship test, you're in the "citizenship grant" process, which has actually held roughly steady this year. The headline-making backlog is in "proof of citizenship" — a different process for people who are already citizens and need a certificate confirming it.

Two processes, two queues

A citizenship grant is the process for permanent residents becoming Canadian citizens for the first time — the one that includes the knowledge test, the language requirement, and eventually the oath ceremony. As of mid-2026, roughly 320,000 people are in this queue, with processing estimated around 13 months.

A proof of citizenship certificate is different: it's for people who are already citizens under Canadian law but need official documentation of that status — often used for a passport application or to formally confirm citizenship by descent. This queue has grown dramatically in 2026, now sitting close to 100,000 people with waits stretching toward 19 months.

Why proof of citizenship suddenly spiked

The driver is Bill C-3, which took effect in December 2025 and removed the previous first-generation limit on citizenship by descent — opening eligibility to a large number of people, many of them in the United States, who weren't previously considered Canadian citizens by birth. That change alone has added tens of thousands of new applicants to the proof-of-citizenship queue within months.

What this actually means if you're studying for the test

  • Your process (the grant) hasn't seen the same dramatic backlog growth — some 2026 reporting even describes grant timelines improving slightly, even as the proof-of-citizenship side deteriorates rapidly.
  • Don't assume a 19-month figure applies to your application if you're going through the standard naturalization process rather than applying for a certificate confirming existing status.
  • If you genuinely need proof of citizenship urgently (a passport deadline, a job offer, a family emergency), IRCC does have an urgent processing request option for that specific application type — worth knowing about, but it's a different form and process than a citizenship grant application.

The one thing worth doing regardless of which queue you're in

Processing times are estimates recalculated regularly based on current inventory, not fixed promises — so the number attached to your file today may shift by the time a decision actually comes. The one thing within your control while you wait is being genuinely ready: knowing the material cold, having your language requirement squared away if it applies to you, and having your documents in order, so nothing on your end adds delay once your file reaches an officer.
