How to Break Into Digital Marketing With No Experience

A realistic starting path — no marketing degree required — plus a 30-second quiz to find which channel actually fits how you think.

7 min read · Redleaf Study

"Digital marketing" covers such a wide range of genuinely different work — writing, data analysis, ad strategy, community building — that a lot of people trying to break in feel stuck before they even start, unsure which specific direction to actually aim for. The good news: you don't need to master all of it at once, and you don't need a marketing degree to get your first real role.

What Actually Gets Entry-Level Candidates Hired

Digital marketing hiring, more than many fields, weighs a genuine, concrete portfolio heavily — even a small, real campaign you personally ran, with honest results (good or mixed) and real reflection on what you'd change, demonstrates more than a list of completed courses alone. Common entry titles to search for include social media coordinator, content marketing assistant, and SEO or marketing coordinator — roles that typically focus on one channel while you build broader familiarity with the rest.

A Realistic First Project, Even Before Any Job

The single most useful thing you can do before applying anywhere: run a genuinely small, real campaign — for a local business, a personal project, or a volunteer cause — through whichever channel your quiz result pointed toward. Track the honest results, even if they're mixed, and be ready to talk through what you'd change. That concrete experience, however small, is worth more in an interview than describing marketing concepts abstractly.

A word on certifications: they exist and are worth pursuing eventually, but they're not required to start. A certification paired with one real, honest project demonstrates far more than either alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a marketing degree for an entry-level role? ▾
No. Many entry-level roles reward demonstrated understanding and a real portfolio project over a specific formal degree.
What's the fastest way to build a portfolio with no job yet? ▾
Run a small, real campaign for an actual cause — a local business, a personal project, or a volunteer organization — and honestly track and reflect on the real results.
Which channel is genuinely easiest to start with? ▾
There's no single right answer — it depends on your natural strengths. Strong writers often start with content and SEO; people who enjoy data often gravitate toward paid ads and analytics.
